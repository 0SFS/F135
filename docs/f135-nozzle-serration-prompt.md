## What the user wants

The F135's nozzle has three overlapping layers of parts. On the real aircraft
the trailing edge of every layer is serrated (saw-toothed). In the 0SFS model
every trailing edge is straight. The user's description, verbatim:

> the inner and outer engine nozle parts are all serrated […] from the looks of
> p3.jpg it looks like 3 layers of overlapping components, closest to the
> exause you have some thin rectangular metal sheets with a v cutout to make
> the serated, they cover the gap from the center(and from the looks of it
> heaviest component of the nozzle) which appears to be triangle shaped at the
> tip, although the center sections seem to be hollow and are covered up to
> with a metal sheet, it is a stamped metal sheet, to it is there to cover up
> the center(main) component, which has like a weird like hook shape at the tip
> shaped like a v when viewed from top down, u can see it in p3.jpg only the
> innermost ring of components is flat, the center is 3d like a hook at the
> tip, and the outermost is a stamped metal sheet shaped like hill

Read that as three layers, from the gas path outwards. Each has its own review
colour:

| Layer | Review colour | What the user sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inner | **red** | Thin, flat, rectangular sheets with a V cut in the trailing edge. They bridge the gaps between the centre parts. This is the only flat layer. |
| Centre | **blue** | The main, heaviest parts. Each one comes to a triangular point. In section it looks hollow, and its tip is three-dimensional: a hook that reads as a V from above. |
| Outer | **yellow** | Stamped sheet-metal covers over the centre parts, raised along the middle like a hill. They hide the hollow centre parts from outside. |

That reading is the user's, made from photographs. Confirm it against the
photos yourself, and say where you disagree, before you model anything.

## The model as it is now

The nozzle is authored in `F135/scripts/f135Engine/`:

- `profile.json` holds `aperture`, with `segmentCount: 16`, the lengths, half
  widths and thicknesses. Its `qualification` text says the flaps, seals and
  fairings are visual hypotheses.
- `source.mjs` builds the parts, in the loop over the roles `Convergent`,
  `Divergent`, `ConvergentSeal`, `DivergentSeal`, `ConvergentSealShoe`,
  `Fairing` and `FairingSeal`. That is 16 × 7 = 112 rigid parts named
  `F135_<Role>_NN` (see `partName` in `mechanism.mjs`), each with a
  `<name>_Metal` mesh. Each `Divergent` also has a `_Follower`.
- Every part is a straight-edged trapezoid in its own frame. The flaps and
  fairings are closed solids (2 mm and 1.5 mm). The seals are single sheets
  (0.7 mm). The fairing has a slot for its follower. No tip has a serration.
- `mechanism.mjs` poses the parts for an aperture from 0 to 1 by rotation and
  translation only (`apertureState`, `partPose`, `exportedFrames`). The seals'
  long edges stay on the adjacent flap planes.
- `glb.mjs` exports two variants: `full` for the engine test stand and
  `installed` for flight. Both carry the same nozzle. The GLBs have no textures.

A first guess at the mapping, which you must check against the photos and your
renders:

| Layer | Current role | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inner (red) | `DivergentSeal` | Flat sheet between the divergent flaps. |
| Centre (blue) | `Divergent` (+ `Follower`) | The heavy flap. In the photo it has depth and a hooked tip; in the model it is a flat 2 mm plate. |
| Outer (yellow) | `Fairing` | The external flap over each divergent flap. |
| ? | `FairingSeal` | The model has a seal between the fairings. Find out what, if anything, it is in the photos before you decide its colour and shape. |

The convergent parts and the throat shoe are inside, out of sight. Leave them
alone unless a render shows they are visible.

Things that depend on the nozzle and must stay correct:

- **The airframe closeout.** `scripts/f135Engine/closeout.mjs` shapes the
  airframe's aft closeout round the installed engine's nozzle, swept through
  pitch 0–95°, yaw ±10° and aperture 0–1, with a margin of 1.5 cm. It runs in
  the same build. If a serrated part reaches further out, the closeout moves
  out with it, so the airframe GLB will change too. Its guards fail loudly if
  AF267's opening no longer matches. Read
  `F-35B/docs/proposals/f35b-aft-fuselage-closeout.md` first; it records why the
  closeout is the shape it is.
- **The exhaust.** The plume, its optics and the engine-exhaust tests in fly
  use the nozzle's exit (`exitRadius` and `exitZ` from `apertureState`). Add the
  serrations as tip geometry and keep the throat and exit stations as they are.
  If you find you must change them, stop and ask first.
- **The app's own rules.** Follow the organization `AGENTS.md` on materials,
  the exhaust depth pass, shadows, hidden preparation and render-on-demand. The
  parts keep their current materials (`F135_LinerHot` inside, `F135_Outer`
  outside) unless the user agrees otherwise. Do not add textures, alpha cut-outs
  or morph targets: the serrations are geometry on rigid parts.
- **Size.** The installed engine has 12,976 triangles and the airframe 12,231.
  Report how many triangles you add to each variant, and keep the addition in
  proportion to what it shows.
- **Counts.** The rig test expects 112 rigid parts. If the photos show a tooth
  count other than 16 per layer, that is a change to the mechanism, the
  closeout and the exhaust. Present it to the user as a decision, with the
  evidence, and do not make it yourself.

## Review images

Make colour-coded renders like
`F-35B/docs/proposals/images/aft-fuselage-closeout/changes-rear.png`:
**yellow** for the outer layer, **blue** for the centre and **red** for the
inner, and neutral greys for everything else. Put each render beside the photo

- the views of p3, p2, p5, p6 and p10, parked (pitch 0) or in hover (pitch 95)
  as the photo shows;
- the view from directly behind, to compare tooth counts with p1;
- one segment on its own, from above, from the side and from behind, to show
  each tip's shape, including the centre part's hook and V;
- apertures 0, 0.5 and 1 from behind and from the side.

A software rasteriser that does this already exists, as scratch from the
closeout work, in `F135/build/closeout/`:

- `raster.mjs` renders flat-shaded orthographic views and writes PNG sheets.
- `scene.mjs` loads the airframe and the engine and poses them with the
  production rig.
- `models.mjs` colours the triangles.
- `doc-views.mjs` and `render-views.mjs` have the photo-matched camera views and
  sheet layouts.

Run them from that directory with `node --import ./register.mjs <script>`.
`register.mjs` resolves bare packages from fly's `node_modules`, because F135
has none. Copy what you need into `F135/build/serration/` rather than editing
these files, so the closeout's images can still be reproduced. Colour the
nozzle by mesh name, taking care that `F135_Divergent_` is also a prefix of
`F135_DivergentSeal_`.

## Seeing a candidate in the app

fly has a dev-only model override. `?modelOverride=<set>` loads every asset a
set lists from `fly/build/model-overrides/<set>/` instead of `public/`, in the
flight view and on the engine test stand (`?engineTest=1`). fly's README has the
details. Work from `fly/`:

1. Before you change anything, save the current models as the baseline:

   ```sh
   npm run model-override -- before-serration aircraft/f-35b/F-35B_AF267-airframe.glb aircraft/f-35b/engine/F135-PW-600-installed.glb aircraft/f-35b/engine/F135-PW-600-full.glb
   ```

2. To build a candidate without installing it, run
   `node ../F135/scripts/build-f135-engine.mjs` with no flag. It writes the
   three GLBs to a new `build/assets/f135-engine/<stamp>/` and leaves `public/`
   alone.
3. Make a set from that output, giving all three files, since the airframe's
   closeout follows the nozzle:

   ```sh
   npm run model-override -- serration-a \
     aircraft/f-35b/F-35B_AF267-airframe.glb=build/assets/f135-engine/<stamp>/F-35B_AF267-airframe.glb \
     aircraft/f-35b/engine/F135-PW-600-installed.glb=build/assets/f135-engine/<stamp>/F135-PW-600-installed.glb \
     aircraft/f-35b/engine/F135-PW-600-full.glb=build/assets/f135-engine/<stamp>/F135-PW-600-full.glb
   ```

4. Give the user the addresses: `/fly/?modelOverride=serration-a` and
   `/fly/?engineTest=1&modelOverride=serration-a`, with
   `?modelOverride=before-serration` to compare.

An override replaces GLBs only. The app still uses the generated
`F135/src/generated/f135EngineData.ts` that is installed. A candidate that
changes `profile.json`'s geometry or aperture values is therefore only shown
faithfully once installed with `npm run build:engine-assets`.

## Stages

### 1. Read, count and propose. Then stop.

- Read the files above and the closeout proposal.
- Crop p3, p2, p5, p6 and p10 to the nozzle, from the full-size originals where
  you have them.
- Count the teeth in each layer, and say how sure you are of each count.
  Estimate each tooth's depth and angle relative to its part's length and
  width, from the photo with the least foreshortening.
- Render the current model in the three colours, beside the photos.
- Write down, with annotated crops:
  - the mapping from layers to parts;
  - the shape you propose for each tip, as a sketch or a profile drawing;
  - what you will not model, and why.

**Stop.** Show the user the crops, the counts, the renders of the current model
and the proposal. Ask them to confirm the mapping and the shapes.

### 2. Prototype one segment in scratch. Then stop.

- Build the agreed shapes for one segment in `F135/build/serration/`, as
  replacements for those parts' geometry, without changing production source.
- Show the segment from the views in [Review images](#review-images), at
  apertures 0, 0.5 and 1.
- Check that the seals still sit on the adjacent flaps, and that no part passes
  through another at any aperture.

**Stop.** Show the user, and ask for corrections.

### 3. Put it in the source, build and check. Then stop.

- Move the agreed geometry into `source.mjs`, and into `profile.json` if it
  needs new dimensions. Keep the parts rigid. Keep their names, roles, frames
  and the 112-part count unless the user agreed to change them.
- Build a candidate (no flag), make the `serration-a` set, then install with
  `npm run build:engine-assets`.
- Run the checks below, and render the full review set again.

**Stop.** Show the user the renders, the triangle counts and the check results,
and give them the in-app addresses.

### 4. Document.

- Update `F135/public/aircraft/f-35b/engine/NOTICE.md` and the `qualification`
  text in `profile.json`, to say the serrations are a visual approximation
  fitted to photographs.
- If the airframe changed, say so in `F-35B/public/aircraft/f-35b/NOTICE.md`
  and keep AF267's CC BY 4.0 credit.
- Write a short record in `F135/docs/proposals/` with the photos, the renders
  and what you measured.

## Checks

Run from `fly/` and keep each one short:

- `npm run verify:engine-assets`, after installing.
- `node --import ../F135/build/closeout/register.mjs ../F135/scripts/validation/f35b/check-f135-clearance.mjs`.
  It takes about ten seconds. Today it reports `passed-with-preserved-closed-source-seam`:
  150 cases, no failures, no engine–airframe hits, no gross
  self-intersections. The only door hits are at pitch 0, on the fuselage's
  closed seam (5.7 mm). Any new hit, or a hit on the closeout, is a failure to
  fix rather than to explain. The bearing seam contacts are allowed, but report
  the count before and after.
- `npx vitest run ../F135/scripts/build-f135-engine.test.mjs`. It holds the
  112-part rig test, the closeout's triangle counts and the aft casing seam.
- `npx vitest run src/flight/aircraft/`, which covers the engine exhaust,
  the engine visuals, the model loader and the bay doors.
- If the airframe changed, also run `src/flight/aircraft/f35bBayDoors.test.ts`,
  `../stores/tests` and `src/flight/jsbsim/f35bStores.integration.test.ts`, and
  say whether any fit moved.
- `npm run typecheck` and `npm run lint` if you touched TypeScript.
